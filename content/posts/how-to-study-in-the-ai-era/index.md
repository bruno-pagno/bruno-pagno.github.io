As a software engineer, even the way I study has changed. Before the AI era, it was extremely important to be proficient in a programming language: know all its details, how to use it, common errors, patterns, and so on. It was common to learn by watching videos from learning platforms or YouTube, follow along as someone coded, post questions when you got stuck on an error, and ask for help.

Nowadays, this seems pointless, since this is exactly the type of thing AI can solve with mastery. What's the goal of learning syntax and coding by hand if you're not going to actively use it in your job or personal projects?

I'm still developing my way of studying, but I've definitely changed what I used to do. As I mentioned in ["My Development Workflow in the AI Era"]({{< ref "my-development-workflow-on-ai-era" >}}), a bigger part of my job now is reviewing code, whether generated by AI or written by teammates, so I study to get better at that: I use Claude to generate pieces of code for me to review and understand, along with reading the documentation for the tools I'm using. I even spin up small projects to test concepts, since that's become extremely cheap to do now.
